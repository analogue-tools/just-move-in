## Primary research

I fielded a 15 question survey to recent movers, "Just Moved In?", in July 2026. Five quantitative questions, two on preferred mechanics, seven open. It exists to answer three things desk research could not: whether people would actually hand this over, whether they want one tap approval or a voice call, and what the settling phase feels like to someone not motivated enough to write a review.

The two questions that carry the most weight are **which single task gave you the biggest headache**, which tests whether my must-do ordering is right, and **would you prefer 1-tap approval, a 2-minute voice call, or doing it manually**, which tests the modality split directly. Full instrument, analysis plan written before the data, and results in the accompanying survey document.

**Two caveats I would state before quoting any number from it.** The trust questions are positively primed, describing the assistant finding the best deals in under 60 seconds, so stated intent there is directional rather than demand validation. And the sample runs through my own network, which skews younger and more digitally comfortable than the mover population, cutting in favour of the agent.

**Honest caveat.** 60 complaints against roughly 3,700 reviews is not a crisis rate. These themes tell me what to design for. They do not tell me how often it happens, and I would not present them as if they did. The 4 star reviews, which I do not have, are where the cheapest fixes usually live.

## The problem

Your five star reviews describe a service that takes the stress away. A meaningful slice of the one star reviews describe a service that added stress on top of a move, sometimes with financial consequences, to people who never asked to be contacted. Both are true, and they describe different parts of the same company: **the conversation, and everything around it.**

> **How do we make the best version of the home setup call the guaranteed minimum for every mover, across 30 items and six weeks, with the mover able to see at any moment what has actually happened rather than what was promised?**

**The strategic consequence.** Automating the call would be automating the part that already works. The product is the execution layer and the receipts.

## Four cells are empty across the whole market

1. Nobody manages lead times and dependencies.
2. Nobody survives a move date change.
3. Nobody is there on moving day.
4. **Nobody can tell a mover whether a notification actually landed.**

The moat is a data trigger plus integration rails, not a model. Anyone can buy the same models next quarter. Nobody can buy your partner feed.

## What I am deliberately not doing

- **No bills bundling.** Orchestrate the setup, stay out of the invoice.
- **No comparison table.** It is a measured conversion penalty, not a style preference.
- **No general chatbot as the front door.** Every competitor is building that. It differentiates nothing.
- **No voice for the whole move.** Voice leads in banking and telco because password resets are scoped intents. It lags in healthcare and travel on emotional handling and edge cases. A move looks like the second group. It is also, in your reviews, currently associated with unsolicited sales calls.
- **No removing humans.** 35 of 39 positive reviews name a person. That is the asset we are scaling, not the cost we are cutting.
- **No direct to consumer.** Updater tried it from 2011 and failed until they pivoted to the agent channel.

## Where voice leads and where UI leads

Access decides this, not taste.

| Phase | What they physically have | Lead |
|---|---|---|
| t-30 | Everything | **Neither.** Ambient only |
| t-14 | Desk, 20 to 50 minutes | **UI leads, voice explains** |
| Moving day | No broadband, one free hand | **Voice leads, UI optional** |
| t+14 | Mobile, low attention | **UI leads, ambient** |

**Rule: voice for explaining and capturing, UI for deciding and confirming.** Never take a financial commitment by voice, because commission disclosure has to be seen to be auditable. And given that voice currently arrives in your reviews as an unsolicited sales call, the AI version has to be outbound on an agreed slot, or it inherits that reputation.

## Impact

**North star: Critical Path Completion.** The share of moves where every lead-time-critical task is done before it becomes impossible or expensive. A customer outcome, not an engagement proxy, and it cannot be gamed by making the app stickier.

| Watch | Guard against |
|---|---|
| Tasks completed per move: 6 to 8 today, 15 in v1 | Complaint rate, and FCA reportable complaints |
| **Share of tasks that reach a confirmed receipt** | **Share of confirmed states later proven false** |
| Date changes absorbed with zero re-entry | Cancellation within 14 days of a switch |
| Time to closure: 50 minutes today, under 10 | Re-presenting a declined recommendation, which must be zero |
| Renewal attach at 12 months | Time to a human for vulnerability-flagged movers |
| NPS at day +3 and +14 | Contact attempts before opt-out is honoured |

**First four experiments:** one recommendation versus three on your own users; exchange versus offer as the trigger; disclosure on versus off, where **I expect to lose a little conversion and I want to know how much**; and the Day 0 voice touch versus none.

## Rollout

Six phases, with gates and kill criteria agreed before we start rather than negotiated afterwards.

| Phase | What ships | Cohort | Commercial logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 · Receipts** | Move record, task lifecycle states, status visibility. **No AI** | Everyone, on the existing human service | Fixes the biggest complaint theme immediately. Value lands even if the agent slips |
| **1 · Shadow** | Agent runs in parallel, output never shown to a customer | Internal only | Zero customer risk. Buys the accuracy data the business case needs |
| **2 · The unreached** | Full agent flow, self serve | Movers who never answer the phone | **Pure upside.** These moves are worth nothing today, so nothing can be cannibalised |
| **3 · Design partner** | Agent led with human escape | One named letting agent, opt in | Contained blast radius, with a partner who agreed to be a test bed |
| **4 · Day zero voice** | Two intents: keys, meter capture | Phase 3 cohort | Highest emotional stakes, so it goes last on a stable base |
| **5 · Scale** | Everything | Partner book, staged | Only after unit economics are proven on a real cohort |

**The two decisions I would defend hardest.**

*Phase 0 ships no AI.* Putting an agent on top of a process where notifications go out seven days after the move and nobody can see anything does not fix it, it scales it. Phase 0 addresses 18 of 60 complaints with no model involved, builds the exact rails the agent needs, and means a two quarter delay to the AI still leaves the business better off.

*Phase 2 starts with movers who never answer the phone.* They produce no revenue today, so the baseline is zero, anything converted is incremental, and it removes the argument that would otherwise dominate every review meeting about whether the agent converts worse than a human.

**Stop condition.** If the agent takes a wrong-property action that reaches a supplier, the programme pauses for review. That failure lands on credit files rather than on convenience, and the current service already produces it in 9 of 60 negative reviews.

**Testing** runs in six layers: contract tests against every partner sandbox, a dedicated cascade regression suite asserting nothing is ever lost on a date change, 200 historical moves replayed through the agent, live shadow mode, adversarial testing on prepayment meters and landlord-liable tenancies and prompt injection, and an external accessibility audit. Move Specialists run UAT and hold a veto on recommendation quality, which is both the sharpest quality filter available and a change management mechanism.

**The people part, which is the hard part.** Move Specialists are told in week 0, before any partner or customer. In writing: this is not a headcount reduction in year one. Production agents resolve 38 to 50% of contacts, and offering access to a human raised uptake 24% in the credit union study even though almost nobody used it. Roughly half of movers will still touch a person. The role changes from running every call to owning a book of moves as a named accountable person, handling escalations, complex tenures and vulnerable customers. **Specialist attrition is a tracked programme metric with a threshold, not an HR footnote.** If we get this wrong the reviews stop naming people, and the thing customers actually love disappears before the replacement is ready.

**If the plan has to be cut,** cut phases 4 and 5. A mover who can see that their council notification was sent on the 2nd, is being chased on the 16th, and cannot be confirmed because Camden does not confirm to third parties, is a mover who does not write the review saying they were told it was handled and it was not.

## Handoff

**A single-file HTML prototype plus a written behavioural spec. No Figma.**

Figma serves two of three audiences. A customer cannot really use one on a phone, and an engineer gets pixels without behaviour. The hard parts here are behavioural. Figma can draw `confirmed` and `sent, no receipt`. It cannot express the rule that a task may only reach `confirmed` if a receipt is stored, which is a database constraint.

You mentioned your engineers have had trouble with HTML from Claude, so it is built to be read: no framework, no build step, CSS custom properties as the literal token layer, class names matching the component inventory. The CSS is meant to be lifted. There is an **Engineering notes** toggle that overlays component names, states and the reasoning inline.

**Good enough means** an engineer can build the whole flow without asking a designer one question about spacing, colour, type or state. The differentiation here is mostly restraint, and restraint specifies well.

**Where it needs another designer, honestly:** illustration and the could-do tier once it grows past eight tiles, the voice persona and script, and a real WCAG 2.2 AA audit. The last two I would treat as blocking, because failure there is invisible to us and expensive to the people least able to absorb it, and disabled movers appear unprompted in your own reviews as both the segment who need this most and the segment carrying the worst downside when it goes wrong.

## AI use

Claude for search, synthesis and the build. It was genuinely good at coding 99 reviews for mechanism rather than sentiment, holding an evidence chain together, and producing a precise front end. My judgment did the work on: re-anchoring the phases to events instead of dates, refusing the bundling revenue, keeping the humans, mapping tone to task tier instead of building two interfaces, and deciding that visible state is worth more than a clean green dashboard.

**The thing I got wrong first time.** Working only from the positive reviews, I concluded the failure mode was inconsistent follow-up. The negative sample shows it is structural: a seven week window in which nobody, including Just Move In, knows what has actually happened. That correction is the reason this direction is about receipts rather than conversation.
