# Primary Research: Mover Survey
### "Just Moved In?" · fielded July 2026 · Jeanne Piffaut

Wiki home: [`docs/wiki/00-INDEX.md`](docs/wiki/00-INDEX.md). Notion skim: [`docs/CASE-STUDY.md`](docs/CASE-STUDY.md).


> **Status:** Results below from the fuller July 2026 analysis (**n = 12**). Directional only. Treat shares as signal, not proof.

---

## 1. Why I ran it

The brief said primary research is a plus. It also happens to be the thing my desk research could not give me. I had two Trustpilot samples, one overwhelmingly positive and one overwhelmingly negative, and nothing from a mover who was not motivated enough to write a review. That middle group is most movers.

Three things I specifically could not answer from secondary sources:

1. **Would people actually hand this over?** Every trust statistic I found was about AI shopping agents in general, not about admin delegation during a move.
2. **One tap or voice?** My whole modality argument rests on the claim that people want to see commercial decisions and only use voice when they have no screen. That is an inference from access conditions, not an observation.
3. **What does the emotional arc actually look like?** The settling phase evidence came from a handful of published move diaries, which self-select for people who write.

---

## 2. The instrument, as fielded

15 questions. Five quantitative, two on preferred mechanics, seven open.

| # | Question | Type | What decision it tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | When did you last move home, and how far | Choice: local, relocation, international | Segmentation. Distance changes the council and supplier picture entirely |
| 2 | How stressful was the administrative side, 1 to 10 | Scale | Calibrates the stress finding against a non-review population |
| 3 | Which single task gave you the biggest headache | Choice: council tax, broadband, finding suppliers, address updates | **Directly tests my ordering.** I ranked broadband first on lead time. If council tax or finding suppliers dominates, my must-do order is wrong |
| 4 | How many separate calls or forms | Choice: 1 to 3, 4 to 8, 9 to 15+ | Sizes the fragmentation claim in the mover's own count rather than in a checklist |
| 5 | Would you trust a digital assistant to notify the council, set up water and present 3 broadband deals | Choice: yes with a summary, only with human escape, no | **Tests the trust ceiling** and, critically, how many need the human option |
| 6 | How would you prefer to complete it: 1-tap approval, 2-minute voice call, or manual | Choice | **The single most decision-relevant question in the survey.** Tests the UI decides, voice explains split directly |
| 7 | What was the first thing you did once you had the keys, any rituals | Open | Day 0 content. Whether the ritual layer is real outside Mumsnet |
| 8 | One thing you wish someone had handed you on Day 1 | Open | Day 0 card contents |
| 9 | Walk me through the day your move date was confirmed, and the next 24 hours | Open | **Tests the activation trigger.** Do people act on exchange, or earlier, or not at all |
| 10 | What did you leave latest, and why | Open | Procrastination ordering, which drives nudge priority |
| 11 | Tell me about something that went wrong, what did it cost you | Open | Validates or refutes the cost of delay ledger in real money |
| 12 | When did it start to feel like home, what made that happen | Open | The settling phase and the could-do tier |
| 13 | If a service had offered to do all this, what would have stopped you trusting it | Open | **The objection list.** What the referral screen has to answer |
| 14 | What local information or tools would have been most valuable in week one | Choice: room planner, bin days and council guides, local community, housewarming discounts | Prioritises the could-do tier and tests whether the affiliate surface is wanted or resented |

Questions 9 to 13 are the five I had written into the research plan as interview questions, asked verbatim so the survey and any follow-up calls are comparable.

## 3. Analysis plan

Written before the data, so the conclusions are not retrofitted.

| Question | Result that confirms my direction | Result that forces a change |
|---|---|---|
| Q3 biggest headache | Broadband leads | If council tax or finding suppliers leads, reorder the must-do list and move Tier 2 notifications up the roadmap |
| Q4 calls and forms | Most say 9 to 15+ | If most say 1 to 3, the fragmentation argument is weaker than I have claimed and the pitch needs rebuilding around decision quality rather than volume |
| Q5 trust | A majority accept it with a summary to approve | If "no, I prefer doing it manually" leads, the product needs a visible manual mode and the agent becomes an assistant, not an operator |
| Q5 human escape | A meaningful share pick "only if I can speak to a human" | This is the expected result. It would confirm the persistent human affordance is load bearing rather than decorative |
| Q6 mechanics | 1-tap approval leads, voice second | If voice leads, my modality split is wrong and voice should extend into discovery, not just Day 0 |
| Q9 next 24 hours | People act on confirmation | If people describe doing nothing for weeks, the activation trigger is right but the nudge has to work harder |
| Q13 trust objections | Objections cluster on commission, accuracy and control | Novel objections go straight onto the referral screen as things it must answer |
| Q14 week one tools | Bin days and council guides lead, community second | If housewarming discounts lead, the affiliate surface is more welcome than I assumed and could-do can be monetised harder |

## 4. Results

**n = 12.** Some questions have fewer answers where people skipped; I cite the true denominator each time. Small, so everything here is directional, not significant. I am reporting it as signal to act on, not proof. Where the number is too small to lean on, I say so.

### 4.1 Sample
- **10 local moves, 1 relocation, 2 international** (one respondent counted an international move). Skews local, which suits the council and utility focus.
- Distributed through my own network, so younger and more digitally comfortable than the mover population. That cuts in favour of the assistant, so I discount positive trust answers accordingly.
- Also grounded in **my own move experience** (16+ moves across countries) and directed storytelling with 2 people.

### 4.2 Quantitative

| Question | Result | Read against my assumption |
|---|---|---|
| **Stress, admin side** | Mean **5.3**, median **6.5**, and a hard split: five people at 7 or above, three at 1 to 3 | Bimodal, not uniformly high. Some movers breeze through admin and some find it acute. The product is for the top half; stop implying everyone is drowning |
| **Biggest headache** | **Tied. Address updates across banks/HMRC/DVLA (4) and finding current energy/water suppliers (4)**, then council tax (2) | **This refutes my ordering.** I ranked broadband first on lead time. Movers name address-change sprawl and supplier discovery as worse |
| **Calls and forms** | Evenly split: five at 1 to 3, five at 4 to 8, two at 9 to 15+ | Fragmentation held for half the sample and was overstated for the other half. Pitch should not lead with a volume number |
| **Trust** | **5 yes with a summary, 5 only with a human escape, 2 no**. So 10 of 12 would delegate, but half of those attach a human condition | Confirms the direction and confirms the human escape hatch is load bearing, not decorative. Only 2 hard refusals |
| **Preferred mechanic** | **6 of 10 chose one-tap Approve All.** 2 manual, 1 wanted a human option, 1 wanted more than three deals | **Strongest result.** Backs UI-decides. One-tap beat voice; nobody chose voice as first preference |
| **Week-one tools** | Bin days and council guides (3), then a three-way tie: community, housewarming discounts, and a 2D room and furniture planner | Room planner appearing unprompted is a genuine surprise and a could-do idea I had not considered |

### 4.3 What the open answers actually said

Coded for mechanism, the way I coded the Trustpilot samples.

**The "wish I'd been handed on day 1" question maps almost perfectly onto the product.** Two people wrote, unprompted, that they wanted the utilities completely set up with no account creation, described as "all that nonsense". One wrote a near-exact spec for the transparency card: "a list of all the necessary info, utilities options, WiFi options, TV options, a list of all the things you need to change your details on. Which council we fall under, the band." That is the plan screen, described by a mover who had never seen it.

**Two independent stories validate the cost-of-delay ledger in real money.** One person could not vote in the council elections because they updated their polling address too late. Another had no broadband for two weeks. A third was without a bed for a week because deliveries could not reach the new address. These are the exact consequences the must-do list exists to prevent.

**One answer is the single best argument for the "issues to fix before you move in" idea.** A respondent wanted "a complete plan of the apartment, all electrical, water and network wiring AND all identified issues to be fixed," having discovered a limestone-blocked toilet valve that cost about £100 and a router that would not connect to the ISP box. Their phrase: fix them "before we find out ourselves by chance."

**The moving-day ritual is real and worth protecting.** Asked what they did first with the keys: "lie on the floor and feel the place," "take a video, sit on the sofa, take in that this is my new home," champagne, a beer, first tea from the favourite cup. This is the joy layer the product must not trample with admin.

**"Felt like home" clusters at weeks to months, via small acts of belonging:** a rug in the living room, tea on the balcony, decorating, "belongings on show," settling into a routine. That confirms the could-do tier should be about belonging, not admin.

### 4.4 Trust objections, verbatim themes

Q13 is the objection list the referral screen has to answer. Coded:

| Objection | Count | What the screen must do |
|---|---|---|
| Data privacy, security, where it goes | 4 | Show what data is held and let the mover see where it went. One asked directly for "visibility of where it is going" |
| Ability to edit the data it holds | 1 | Let the mover correct any pre-filled field |
| Impartiality, who owns the tech | 1 | Commission disclosure and panel scope. This person said they would "do my own research first" |
| Prefer a human involved | 1 | The named human, already core |
| Anti-AI on principle (environment) | 1 | Will not be won. Acceptable to lose |

**The privacy cluster is the headline.** Four of twelve named data handling as the blocker, which outranks cost. Referral and Settings need a plain answer to "what do you have and where does it go." Audit log stops being a nice-to-have.

### 4.5 What the survey changed

- **Confirmed:** one-tap over voice, the human escape hatch, the cost-of-delay consequences, the settling-phase belonging thesis, and demand for "set it all up so I don't have to."
- **Refuted:** broadband as the number one headache. Address-change sprawl and supplier discovery beat it. Reordered must-do messaging accordingly.
- **Surprised me:** the 2D room and furniture planner, named by several people, and the strength of the privacy objection over cost.

## 5. Honest notes on the instrument

I would rather flag these than have them raised for me.

**Q5 and Q6 are primed.** Both describe the assistant doing the work "in under 60 seconds" and finding "the 3 best deals". That is a flattering frame, and stated intent about a hypothetical product is weakly predictive. Treat a positive result as directional, not demand validation.

**Q5's option set is the useful part.** Because one option is explicitly "only if I can speak to a human if something goes wrong", the question separates outright refusal from conditional acceptance. That distinction matters more than the headline yes rate.

**Two things I should have asked and did not.**
First, nothing about **how they were contacted**. Given that 16 of the 60 negative Trustpilot reviews are about unsolicited contact and consent, that is now a first order issue and the survey is silent on it.
Second, nothing about **app versus web versus text**. Whether movers would install anything is a live product question and this instrument cannot answer it.

**Tenure is inferred, not asked.** Q1 captures distance, not whether the respondent rents or owns.

**Sample bias.** Own network: younger, more digitally comfortable, more London-weighted than the actual mover population. Discount positive trust results accordingly.

## 6. What I would do next with more time

1. **Five follow-up directed storytelling sessions** with respondents who gave the most detailed open answers, using questions 9 to 13 as the spine.
2. **Re-ask Q6 as a prototype task** rather than a stated preference. Put the basket screen in front of people and see whether they tap approve or ask to talk to someone.
3. **A consent question**, fielded separately, on how people want to be contacted after a letting agent passes their details on.
