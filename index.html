Feature

Referral

The problemPeople arrive from a referral they did not ask for, into a sale that may still collapse.
The designReferring agent named up top. Continue, Pause, Opt out as equal-weight choices. Zero outbound contact until exchange (or equivalent).
The evidence37% of agreed sales did not complete in 2025 (Connells). 16 of 60 Trustpilot negatives (≤3★ sample) cite unwanted contact or consent wounds. Survey Q5 (n=12 of 12, directional): 5 summary / 5 human escape / 2 no; human path load-bearing.
Road not takenNo chatbot front door. It is what every competitor builds and it differentiates nothing here.
Feature

Home

The problemMovers lose the plot: where they are, what is next, and what they authorised a third party to do.
The designHome as stage hub. One dominant next action. Signed permission as a short readable status, not a legal wall.
The evidenceSurvey Q13: control and data reliability objections (“Inability to individually edit some of the personal data it has used”; “Privacy, safety, costs”). Trustpilot commercial opacity theme: 13 of 60.
Road not takenNo dense dashboard of stats and promos on first read. Anxiety needs an anchor, not a feed.
Feature

Discovery

The problemHousehold facts get retyped at every utility checkout, while the real pain is missed outcomes and weak decisions, not form theatre.
The designShort tap questions collected once. Answers feed energy, broadband, and contents ranking. Ask Jay stays a side channel.
The evidenceSurvey: n=12 responses. Q6 answered by 10: 6 chose 1-tap; voice never first. Q4 split across 1-3 / 4-8 / 9-15+. Own-network, directional.
Road not takenNo chatbot as the main way to answer. No one-question-at-a-time flow in v1 (parked for A/B after instrumentation).
Feature

Basket

The problemComparison grids freeze movers. Opaque “best deals” look paid. Prepaid meters and medical needs are hard failure modes.
The designOne recommended option per category with short reasons. Alternatives behind arrows. Same panel fee stated once. Safety gates can block confirm and escalate to a human.
The evidenceOfgem trials: single offer converts about 14 to 29.5%; three or more about 2.4 to 13.4%. Quad/Harris pattern: trust drops when AI picks look paid. Trustpilot commercial opacity 13/60; vulnerability near-miss in sample. Survey Q6: 1-tap led.
Road not takenNo equal comparison grid. No bills bundling. No hiding the fee inside the unit price.
Feature

Confirmed

The problem“Told it was handled” when it was not is how trust dies after the sale moment.
The designTwo lists: Jay is handling / Needs you next. Sent is not confirmed. Snapshot only; Home stays the hub.
The evidence18 of 60 Trustpilot negatives (≤3★ sample): told handled, was not; often discovered via bill or debt letter. Theme confidence high in-sample; not a prevalence rate across all reviews.
Road not takenNo fake all-green success screen. No second competing home dashboard.
Feature

Pre-move

The problemBetween confirm and move day, work goes invisible. Councils and water can take weeks with no receipt.
The designYour tasks first. Jay’s work visible below. Waiting states say sent · no receipt with a chase date.
The evidenceBuell & Norton: people preferred services that visibly signalled effort, even when results were identical. JMI’s own replies: notifications about seven days after move date; councils/water up to six weeks. Survey open answers: “All the subscriptions to the utilities solved”; “Guide for each process.”
Road not takenNo collapsing Jay’s work behind a toggle. No green ticks without a stored receipt.
Feature

Move-in day

The problemKeys and meters happen with limited attention. Day 0 is rest and ritual; forced voice is the wrong default.
The designLarge Yes / No always on screen. Voice optional. Meters and deposit photos follow. Soft welcome extras never block must-dos.
The evidenceSurvey n=12; Q6 (n=10): voice never first preference. Day 0 open answers: “Lie on the floor and feel the place”; “take a video, sit on the sofa…”; first tea from the favourite cup. Directional.
Road not takenNo voice-only Day 0. No admin wall in the doorway before keys.
Feature

Post-move

The problemIf the product dies after keys, week-one usefulness and stickiness never start.
The designBins, guides, small tasks. Skippable survey. Clear path into Market. No hard sell.
The evidenceSurvey Q14: among those who answered, bin/council guides, neighbourhood/community, and housewarming discounts tied. Habit window desk research (~3 months post-move).
Road not takenNo day-one ad blitz. Sponsored placements wait until after experience (Later).
Feature

Tasks

The problemThree views that become three truths. Phone needs a default that still shows ownership and status.
The designOne task store. List default. Board and Visual are lenses of the same data. Owner and status on every row.
The evidenceBuell & Norton on operational transparency. Trustpilot praise clusters on “someone handled it”; negatives cluster on invisible failure (18/60 told-handled theme).
Road not takenNo gamified fake progress. No separate status systems per view.
Feature

Date change

The problemMove dates change. Silent drops destroy trust and create specialist rework.
The designPick a new date. Show what moved, what must be redone, and that nothing important was lost. Confirm the summary.
The evidenceConnells: 37% fall-through; offer-to-exchange averages 104 days (up from 76 in 2019). Specialists already multi-touch dates. Hard product rule: lost = 0.
Road not takenNo vague “everything updated” toast. No calendar checklist that assumes a fixed minus-30 day plan.
Feature

Market

The problemMovers cannot tell free NHS/council info from paid partners. Ads too early kill trust.
The designSearch and filters first. Each result labelled free public, local, or panel partner. Fee once at the foot. No fake bookings.
The evidenceSurvey Q14 welcomed guides alongside discounts. Brief: no bills bundling. Checkatrade-style sponsored visibility is a commercial pattern to discuss later, not a v1 chrome copy.
Road not takenNo badge soup. No sponsored placements before trust rules exist.
Feature

Settings

The problemConsent without a real off switch is theatre, especially after unwanted-contact wounds. Default mobile chrome also parks primary taps on the right, which fails left-handed movers and anyone with one free hand on Day 0.
The designPause, opt out, notifications, date change, and signed permission in clear groups. Exits are obvious. Left-handed mode flips the ask bar, compose row, and bottom nav so main taps sit under the left thumb; Yes/No stay LTR.
The evidenceTrustpilot unwanted contact 16/60. Survey control objections (Q13). Roughly one in ten people are left-handed; Day 0 access constraint is one free hand. Kill switches must be honoured in the scheduler, not only in UI.
Road not takenNo decorative toggles that still send messages. No burying opt out. No mirroring the whole UI into Arabic-style RTL as a fake left-hand fix.
Feature

The evidence35 of 39 five-star reviews name a specific Move Specialist. Survey Q5 (n=12 of 12): 5 summary / 5 human escape / 2 no. Human escape hatch research (Kinch & Buell): the path lifts trust even when unused.
