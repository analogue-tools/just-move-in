## In one paragraph

I read the brief and the website, wrote down what I wanted to know and how I would research it, then brain-dumped from intuition. I used AI (Claude Opus, Gemini, Cursor / Grok) step by step with sources to check, took notes on what felt relevant, ran a questionnaire plus directed storytelling, wrote analysis and takeaways, built an HTML prototype from that intuition, critiqued and refined it, and pushed it to Vercel. The demo is the whiteboard and the specs at once: clear enough to collaborate, not a finished product pretending to be definitive.

## Step by step

1. **Brief + website**, notes on what I wanted to know, how I would research, and a brain dump from intuition.
2. **AI-assisted desk research**, ran the brief’s steps one by one with sources to verify. Named models I used for search and critique: Claude Opus, Gemini, Cursor (Grok). Also Google search. Read and kept what I thought was relevant.
3. **Primary research**, questionnaire + directed storytelling; wrote analysis and takeaways. (Detail in Research.)
4. **HTML from the brain dump**, first visual, even when rough, clarified what I wanted from the intuition the research had formed.
5. **Critique and refine**, cycles of research → make → critique → intuition → refine.
6. **Push to Vercel**, shareable object for feedback.

## How I think about this case study

- I collected feedback but wanted to keep my thinking organic. I did not ask for a brainstorm from every available person. I would usually seek diverse perspectives; here I protected a first pass so the thinking stayed mine.
- **The hardest step is the first one.** Once you present something, people start having opinions and articulating them. Design is continuous.
- I built the demo because **clarity of output matters more than me pretending to own every engineering detail.** Engineers are the experts. Designers are the experts. The prototype is a foundation to build together. The designer, product manager, and engineer should interact with it to build true intuition. I believe in co-building. This app acts as the whiteboard and as defined specs. It facilitates object-based learning and collaboration on the creative and engineering side.
- I did not design primarily for internationals first. The industry is saturated there, and my data showed more local transfers. I wanted to lay the foundation for that funnel first. (International kits stay in Later / discuss.)
- I kept asking: **how do we provide more value (and diversify the business model) through the same customer funnel?** Transparency, trust, and making the customer sticky enough to use other services.
- Movers do not know what they do not know. Issues they may come across. What they want. Pre, during, post. No matter how many times you move, it is always different.
- I did not fully land where ads or affiliate placements sit. That is intentional for v1 clarity, and parked under Later / discuss with a commercial intent.
- I did not want to be overly influenced by what Just Move In currently offers or how the existing Figma is laid out. Diversity of perspective matters. Design is continuous and never ending. AI makes a visual language easy to show, which then fosters common ground (object-based learning) to collaborate in real time, like a whiteboard. **What I am presenting is a prototype to collect feedback. It is not definitive.**
